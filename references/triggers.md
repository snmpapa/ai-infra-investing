# Trigger Rules

Use add and reduce triggers instead of vague conviction.

## Add Triggers

Good add triggers usually look like:

- core thesis remains intact, but price corrects
- layer-level demand stays strong
- revenue, margin, or backlog confirms the narrative
- market punishes capex before monetization breaks
- the stock fills a missing portfolio layer

Examples:
- `GOOG`: Cloud strength and AI search do not damage monetization
- `META`: ad growth and profit still validate AI spend
- `NVDA`: capex remains strong and margins hold after a pullback
- `TSM`: packaging demand remains tight
- `MU`: memory tightness and margin expansion continue
- `AMD`: data center CPU and AI system demand keep compounding
- `CRCL`: stablecoin regulation improves and settlement usage expands

## Reduce Triggers

Good reduce triggers usually look like:

- expected demand weakens at the layer level
- margins compress while valuation remains rich
- capex rises but monetization or utilization fails to follow
- a theme position grows too large relative to the portfolio
- a watchlist or trade starts masquerading as a core holding

Examples:
- `GOOG`: AI search hurts ads
- `META`: capex is rewarded less because earnings support weakens
- `NVDA`: hyperscaler capex slows
- `TSM`: geopolitical or packaging logic worsens
- `MU`: pricing cycle rolls over
- `AMD`: CPU optimism outruns actual profit pool
- `HOOD`: activity fades
- `CRCL`: regulatory path worsens

## Trigger Discipline

- A thesis can be right while the position is too large.
- A company can be good while the stock becomes a reduce.
- Always state whether the trigger is:
  - company-specific
  - layer-specific
  - valuation/sentiment-specific
