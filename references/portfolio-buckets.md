# Portfolio Buckets

Use these buckets after classifying the AI stack layer.

## Core Holding

Use for:
- durable business quality
- strong strategic role
- acceptable holding experience
- suitable as a portfolio backbone

Typical examples:
- `GOOG`
- `META`

Typical range:
- low teens to high twenties as a percent of the AI sleeve

## Satellite Holding

Use for:
- important AI stack exposure
- higher volatility or narrower thesis than a core holding
- useful for performance, but not ideal as the main base

Typical examples:
- `NVDA`
- `TSM`
- `MU`
- `AMD`
- `DRAM`

Typical range:
- low single digits to mid teens

## Theme Position

Use for:
- adjacent but less durable narratives
- policy, adoption, payments, or market-activity leverage
- good upside, weaker role as a portfolio backbone

Typical examples:
- `CRCL`
- `HOOD`

Typical range:
- 1% to high single digits

## Defensive Holding

Use for:
- volatility buffer
- diversification against growth-heavy risk

Typical examples:
- `GLD`

Typical range:
- low single digits to low teens

## Watchlist Name

Use for:
- interesting thesis
- insufficient proof
- too much narrative clutter
- redundant with stronger holdings

Typical examples:
- `TSLA`
- weaker second-tier memory names when `MU` or `DRAM` already cover the layer

Typical range:
- 0% to very small tracking size

## Trading-Only High Beta

Use for:
- strong momentum or familiar price zones
- user explicitly treats it as a trade
- not suitable as a base holding

Typical examples:
- `IREN`
- sometimes `CRWV` or `NBIS`
- other high-capex AI infrastructure operators

Typical range:
- small and tightly controlled

## Bucket Rules

- Do not let theme positions crowd out core holdings.
- Do not let multiple holdings with nearly identical stack exposure quietly accumulate.
- If a user frames a name as a wave trade or price-zone trade, default to theme or trading-only unless the business quality argues otherwise.
- Core and satellite are for structured holding.
- Theme and trading-only are for controlled opportunism.
